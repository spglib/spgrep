On-the-fly irreps generation from solvable group chain¶

This page describes an algorithm to generate irreps for solvable groups Neto, 1973.

Subduced and induced representations for solvable group¶

Let \(H\) be invariant subgroup of \(G\) such that \(G/H = \mathbb{Z}_{p}\) (\(p\) is prime and \(r^{p}=e\)),

\[ \begin{aligned} G = \coprod_{m=0}^{p-1} r^{m} H. \end{aligned} \tag{1} \]

Consider projective irrep \(\Delta\) with factor system \(\mu\) for \(H\),

\[ \begin{aligned} S \psi_{j} = \sum_{i=1}^{d} \psi_{i} \Delta(S)_{ij} \quad (R \in H, i = 1, \dots, d). \end{aligned} \]

The following representations are also irrep over \(H\):

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{\Delta}^{(m)}(S) &:= \frac{ \mu(S, r^{m}) }{ \mu(r^{m}, S_{m}) } \mathbf{\Delta}(S_{m}) \quad (S \in H, m = 0, \dots, p-1) \\ S_{m} &:= r^{-m} S r^{m} (\in H) \end{aligned} \]

There are two cases for \(\{ \Delta^{(m)} \}_{m=0, \dots, p-1}\):

  1. They are mutually inequivalent: \(\Delta \uparrow G \downarrow H \cong \sum_{m=0}^{p-1} \Delta^{(m)}\)

  2. They are all equivalent: \(\Delta \uparrow G \downarrow H \cong p \Delta\)

Case-1: conjugated irreps are mutually inequivalent¶

For case-1, the induced representation \(\Delta \uparrow G\) is irrep 1 2:

\[ \begin{aligned} \Delta \uparrow G (r^{m} S)_{i:, j:} &= \mathbb{I}[i \equiv j + m] \frac{ \mu(r^{m}S, r^{j}) }{ \mu(r^{i}, S_{j} ) } \mathbf{\Delta}( S_{j} ) \quad (S \in H).\\ \Delta \uparrow G (S) &= \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{\Delta}^{(0)}(S) & & & \\ & \mathbf{\Delta}^{(1)}(S) & & \\ & & \ddots & \\ & & & \mathbf{\Delta}^{(p-1)}(S) \end{pmatrix} \quad (S \in H) \\ \Delta \uparrow G (r) &= \begin{pmatrix} \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \frac{\mu(r, r^{p-1})}{\mu(E,E)} \mathbf{1} \\ \frac{\mu(r, E)}{\mu(r,E)} \mathbf{1} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \\ \mathbf{0} & \frac{\mu(r, r)}{\mu(r^{2},E)} \mathbf{1} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} \\ \vdots & \vdots & \vdots & & \vdots & \vdots \\ \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \mathbf{0} & \cdots & \frac{\mu(r, r^{p-2})}{\mu(r^{p-1},E)} \mathbf{1} & \mathbf{0} \end{pmatrix}. \end{aligned} \]

Case-2: conjugated irreps are equivalent¶

For case-2, the induced representation \(\Delta \uparrow G\) is reducible. Let one of intertwiner between \(\Delta^{(0)}\) and \(\Delta^{(1)}\) be \(\mathbf{U}\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{\Delta}^{(0)}(S) \mathbf{U} = \mathbf{U} \mathbf{\Delta}^{(1)}(S) \quad (\forall S \in H). \end{aligned} \]

See this page for numerical way to obtain \(\mathbf{U}\). We scale \(\mathbf{U}\) such that \(\mathbf{U}^{p} = \mathbf{1}\).

The induced representation \(\Delta \uparrow G\) is decomposed to \(p\) irreps \(\{ \Delta_{q} \}_{q=0}^{p-1}\),

\[ \begin{aligned} \mathbf{\Delta}_{q}(S) &= \mathbf{\Delta}(S) \quad (S \in H) \\ \mathbf{\Delta}_{q}(r) &= \frac{1}{\omega_{q}} \mathbf{U}. \end{aligned} \]

The coefficient \(\omega_{q} \, (q = 0, \cdots, p - 1)\) is determined as follows:

\[ \begin{aligned} \mu(E, E) \mathbf{1} &= \mathbf{\Delta}_{q}(E) \\ &= \mathbf{\Delta}_{q}(r^{p}) \\ &= \frac{1}{ \prod_{m=1}^{p-1} \mu(r, r^{m}) } \mathbf{\Delta}_{q}(r)^{p} \\ &= \frac{1}{ \prod_{m=1}^{p-1} \mu(r, r^{m}) } \frac{1}{\omega_{q}^{p}} \mathbf{1} \quad (\because \mathbf{U}^{p} = \mathbf{1}) \\ \therefore \omega_{q} &:= \frac{1}{ \left( \mu(E, E) \prod_{m=1}^{p-1} \mu(r, r^{m}) \right)^{\frac{1}{p}} } \exp \left( \frac{2 \pi i q}{p} \right). \end{aligned} \]
